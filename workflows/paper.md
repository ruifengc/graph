# Academic papers

Use with [base.md](base.md). Explain the paper at the depth the reader needs.

- Establish the research question and contribution. Read the abstract and
  figures as an orientation, then inspect the relevant method, experiments,
  results, and limitations before relying on them. Disclose partial access.
- Explain the method or mechanism when it matters to the contribution;
  a result chart alone may not explain the paper. An annotated diagram,
  worked example, or equation can be the central explanation.
- Keep experimental conditions with results: task, dataset, baseline,
  metric direction, sample size or uncertainty where available, and any
  important evaluation assumptions. Do not combine incomparable benchmarks.
- Anchor findings to the PDF page, table, figure, or section and include
  the paper identity/version. Separate reported results, author interpretation,
  and your explanatory analogy.
- Preserve qualifications and negative results that change the conclusion.
  Do not generalize a benchmark gain into universal superiority or treat a
  proposed mechanism as established causality.
- Retain and explain equations when they carry meaning. Use plain language
  around them, define variables, and choose inline notation, MathML, or an
  appropriate renderer for the delivery constraints. Do not reconstruct an
  unavailable formula from a paraphrase.
- When adapting an architecture or mechanism figure, distinguish actual
  components and relationships from explanatory simplifications. Label the
  adaptation and link back to the original.
