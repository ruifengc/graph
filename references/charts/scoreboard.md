# Qualitative comparison

Useful when the source supplies judgments, categories, or mixed findings.

- Attribute verdicts and show the task, conditions, or evidence behind them.
  Preserve mixed or conditional results instead of forcing winners.
- Do not turn “better” into an invented numerical score, bar length, or area.
  Labels or categorical marks can represent a class, not a magnitude.
- If measured scores are available and relevant, include them with their
  context. Qualitative and quantitative evidence can coexist in one table.
- Use clear text as well as color and group rows by subject when useful.
  Source order, a fixed row limit, and a particular chip style are optional.
