# Scatter and dot plots

Use position to compare one or two measured variables per entity.

- Name axes, units, and definitions. For signed data, show meaningful zero
  lines; explain any quadrant boundaries chosen for interpretation.
- Empty regions may be informative; do not populate them for symmetry.
- Axis ranges may focus a cluster when clearly labeled and the treatment
  of outliers is disclosed. Do not imply a length-from-zero encoding when
  the baseline is cropped.
- Put important one-dimensional readings next to their dots when possible.
  In crowded plots, label pivotal entities and provide an accessible way
  to inspect others. Not every point needs a permanent label or a tooltip.
- Separate equal-valued entities or explain their overlap; any jitter is
  for visibility and must not imply a second measured variable.
- Keep zero lines from swallowing row connectors. Flip end labels near
  plot boundaries and derive inspection positions from the same geometry
  as the marks.
- One dot represents an entity or observation here, unlike [unit dots](dots.md).
