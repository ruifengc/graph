# Optional editorial style

A quiet, content-led starting point. Follow the user's brand or project
conventions when present, or choose another coherent direction for the subject.
These values are suggestions, not a closed palette or a validator contract.

## Starting palette

| Role | Light | Dark, if offered |
|---|---|---|
| Background | #F4F3EE | #151515 |
| Primary ink | #1C1C1A | #EDEDEA |
| Secondary text | #625F57 | #B0ADA5 |
| Rules and decoration | #D8D5CB | #393833 |
| Accent | #A33D24 | #E5AC75 |

Use color consistently for meaning. Adapt or extend the palette when more
categories are necessary; combine color with labels, shape, or line style.
Check contrast at the actual size and weight. Decoration colors are not
appropriate defaults for small labels or data marks.

## Type and layout

System sans fonts are a convenient offline default. A serif heading can
provide editorial character. Choose a small, coherent hierarchy rather than
an exact size ladder; keep body copy comfortably readable and verify SVG
labels at their rendered mobile size. Reflow or simplify rather than shrink
important text to fit.

Start with a centered reading column around 55–75 characters for prose,
allowing figures more width when needed. Use whitespace and clear alignment
to separate ideas. Cards, multiple columns, filled regions, and larger type
are options when they express hierarchy; none is mandatory. Let section
height follow its content and use responsive layout instead of screen quotas.

## SVG and motion

CSS variables are convenient for shared colors and themes, but the page
need not use particular variable names. Give diagrams meaningful labels
and accessible descriptions. Preserve geometry when scaling and avoid
clipping or overlapping labels.

Motion is optional. If used, keep it brief enough to support reading and
supply a readable static state. Do not hide core content until a reveal
fires. Reduced-motion preferences suppress motion while retaining controls.

Evidence labels and citations follow their explanatory purpose, not a
particular symbol, class name, or theme.
